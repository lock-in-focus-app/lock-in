02 · The redirect page

A pause before the scroll.

A redirected site doesn't slam a door. It asks for five minutes of focus first. If you still want it after that, go right ahead.

  • Five minutes first. The countdown keeps going while you work somewhere else.
  • Your own if-then plan, written ahead of time: "When I want to scroll, I'll write one paragraph first."
  • Your own links, one click away: the draft, the reading list, whatever you meant to do.
  • Not now takes you back. Still need the site once the wait is up? Take a few minutes on it.

Why it works · Modest, one study

03 · The desktop app

A night sky that fills with your focus.

Between sessions the app's sky is hazy. When you lock in, the haze clears for the whole session, and what you do adds to it. Nothing is ever taken away.

Book the dentist
Look up that song
Your sky Waiting for a session
  1. Stars

    Every session adds a star

    Longer sessions make bigger, brighter stars, in your profile's color: warm gold for Writing, blue-white for Deep Work. Missed days cost nothing.

  2. Constellations

    Each week becomes a constellation

    The week's stars join into a shape that's yours alone, with a made-up name like The Quiet Heron that you can change. A week with gaps is still a constellation.

  3. Planets

    Every month grows a planet

    No two alike: gas giants, ice worlds, lava, marble. It takes shape through the month, from a dusty protoplanet to rings, an atmosphere and, in a big month, an aurora.

  4. Satellites

    Stray thoughts go into orbit

    A thought that pulls at you goes into orbit with Alt+P (Option+P on a Mac), from anywhere, and comes back when the session ends.

  • Apps too

    A profile can close or hide apps like Discord, Steam or Slack during its sessions.

  • Strictness

    Gentle sessions end any time, Medium ones ask you to type a random string first, and Hard ones last until the timer runs out.

  • A quiet timer

    It can end without a sound, and sessions end by themselves when you've walked away.

  • A weekly report

    A quiet look at your week, and backups you can bring to another computer.

  • No extension needed

    On macOS, restricted sites can be shut for every browser and app during a session.

04 · Privacy

Lock In collects nothing.

No accounts, no analytics, no ads, and no servers of its own. Nothing you do in it is sent to us or anyone else.

It stays on your computer

Your profiles, sessions, history and sky. Uninstalling removes them.

It talks only to itself

The extension and the app share over Native Messaging, which never leaves your computer.

You can check every line

Lock In is free and open source under the AGPL, so you can read any of it.

The desktop app

A .dmg for macOS, or a .deb or .AppImage for Linux. It isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer account, so macOS asks you to allow it once: open it, click Done, then System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway.
